Every tool I have kept for more than a few years does one job and stays out of the way. The editor opens fast, the notes sync, and the blog shows what I wrote without asking me to think about servers.

When a tool grows past its job, the cost shows up slowly: a slower start, one more setting, a migration nobody asked for. None of these is large on its own. Together they make writing feel like maintenance.

## What small means

Small is not the same as minimal. A small tool can have a lot inside it, as long as the part you touch every day is quiet. Here is the test I use:

- It starts in under a second.
- I can explain what it does in one sentence.
- Leaving it is a copy, not a project.

The last point matters most. If my words live in files I can read without the tool, I can stay because I want to, not because I have to.
